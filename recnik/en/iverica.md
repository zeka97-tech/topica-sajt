Chipboard is a board of pressed wood particles and glue; melamine-faced chipboard has decor paper bonded onto both faces, so it is ready for carcass and fronts without painting. It is the basic material of panel furniture: sides, bottom, shelves, rails and fronts are made from it, and the edges are closed with [edge banding](./kant.html).

## Standard sizes

- Thickness **18 mm** for carcass and fronts; variants 16, 19 and 25 mm.
- Common sheet sizes: **2800 × 2070 mm** (Egger) or **2750 × 1830 mm** (Kronospan, Kastamonu); the first is about 5.8 m², the second about 5.0 m².
- Backs are not chipboard but **3 mm hardboard (HDF)**.
- Edging **0.45 mm** on the visible carcass edges, **1 mm** on the front.

## In the workshop

The sheet is cut to the cut list, then the edges are banded. A patterned decor has a grain, so decor panels are laid out on the sheet in one direction only, while white chipboard goes whichever way wastes less. A cut without a scoring blade tears the decor on the underside; that is why you cut on a panel saw with a scorer, and if there is none, the visible face goes up. Common mistake: material worked out from the m² of the panels without waste, so half a sheet is missing at the end; how much waste there is depends on how well the panel sizes fit along the long edge of the sheet. Another: sheets from two makers in the “same” white that are not the same under the light.

## How TOPICA handles it

The program tracks material by thickness and decor, adds up the m² of all panels in the [cut list](./krojna-lista.html) and multiplies by the price you entered ([features](../#mogucnosti)); laying the panels out on the sheet (nesting) is not in this version.

The sheet is the same for everyone; the difference is how much of it goes into the carcass and how much into waste.

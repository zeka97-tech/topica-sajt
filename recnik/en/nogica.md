The leg is the adjustable plastic foot a base kitchen unit stands on; it holds the carcass off the floor and gives the height for the plinth. It is adjusted by turning, so a run of units can be levelled even on an uneven floor, and the [plinth](./cokla.html) clips onto the legs.

## Standard sizes

- Height **100 mm**, adjustable in the range **80–150 mm**; the same as the plinth height.
- Number per unit: **4 up to 800 mm wide, 6 above** (program default); the fifth and sixth go under the middle of the bottom.
- Worktop height from the floor ≈ 720 (carcass) + 100 (legs) + 38 (worktop) = **858 mm**; in practice 850–920, to suit the user's height. The other sizes are in the guide [standard kitchen sizes](../blog/standardne-mere-kuhinje.html).

## In the workshop

The legs are screwed to the bottom of the [carcass](./korpus.html), close to the edge of the side so the side carries the weight straight down onto the leg, and set back from the front edge far enough for the plinth to sit behind the front. On installation the two end units of the run are levelled first, then the rest are lined up to a taut string or a long level on the top edge of the carcasses. Common mistake: the legs on the first unit screwed all the way in, so at the other end of the run there is no travel left to reach the same level — you start from the highest point of the floor. Another: a 900 or 1000 mm unit on four legs, so the bottom sags under the weight of the crockery.

![The base run of a kitchen in TOPICA with the plinth in front of the legs, in one line along the run](../slike/web/snimak-39-cokla-1600.webp "The plinth set back behind the front, in line, along the whole run.")

## How TOPICA handles it

The program adds the number of legs by the unit width itself and counts them in the hardware list and the price; you change the plinth and leg height in the project settings and the whole run is recalculated ([features](../#mogucnosti)).

Four legs and ten minutes with a level save an hour of adjusting doors.

The worktop overhang is the part of the [worktop](./radna-ploca.html) that projects past the fronts of the base units, and past the side where there is no wall. It protects the fronts from drips, gives room for the worktop edge and covers the difference between the carcasses and an uneven wall.

## Standard sizes

- Overhang past the front **about 20–30 mm**: carcass 560 + front 18 + overhang ≈ **600 mm**, which is the standard worktop depth.
- At the side, where there is no wall, **20–30 mm** past the side or the end panel.
- Worktop depth **600** (620/635 for deeper carcasses), thickness **38 mm** (28/40).
- Top edge from the floor ≈ 720 + 100 + 38 = **858 mm**; in practice 850–920.

## In the workshop

A wall rarely runs straight: the carcasses are set in a straight line and the worktop is scribed to the wall (a cardboard template or a scribing gauge), and that excess goes into the overhang; that is why the worktop is not cut to exactly 600 until the wall has been measured. See [how to measure a kitchen](../blog/kako-izmeriti-kuhinju.html). Common mistake: a worktop ordered at 600 for a run along a crooked wall, so at one end there is no overhang left and at the other it gapes at the wall. Another: a different overhang on the two runs in a corner, so the worktop joint doesn't meet. A third: the side overhang forgotten next to the fridge or the end panel, so the worktop sits flush with the side and the worktop edge shows in profile.

## How TOPICA handles it

In the program the worktop is built over the selected carcasses and travels with them; the 600 depth follows the 560 carcass and the 18 front, and the worktop and [plinth](./cokla.html) run in one line along the run ([features](../#mogucnosti)).

Two centimetres of overhang are the difference between a worktop that stays dry and one that drips down the fronts.

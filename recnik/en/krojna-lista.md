A cut list is the list of every panel in one piece of furniture with its exact size, thickness, material, edging and quantity. It goes from the workshop to the saw: whoever cuts the sheets doesn't need to know what the piece looks like, the list is enough. If it is right, everything fits; if 18 mm are missing, they are missing in the kitchen too.

## Standard sizes

- The size is **net, after edging** (program default): a side of 702 × 539 is the finished panel; the edging is not added.
- **First dimension = grain direction**: a 717 × 597 front has its grain upright; hardboard is “no grain”.
- Thicknesses: **18 mm** carcass and front, **3 mm** hardboard for the back.
- [Edging](./kant.html): **0.45 mm** visible carcass edges, **1 mm** fronts, **2 mm** heavily used edges; tape length = the sum of the edged edges + **10 % waste**.

## In the workshop

The list is grouped by material and thickness, because that is how it is cut: white 18 mm chipboard first, then the decor fronts, then hardboard. Each line also carries which carcass the panel belongs to, so they don't get mixed up after cutting. Common mistake: a gross size written in the list and the edging added once more, or the two front dimensions swapped so the grain goes horizontal. The example with all eight panels of a 600 × 720 × 560 base carcass, and an explanation of every number, is in the guide [What a cut list is and how to read it](../blog/krojna-lista.html).

## How TOPICA handles it

You don't write the cut list: the program derives it from the carcasses in the room, and the “By size” view puts identical sizes on one line, by material, ready to copy or print to PDF ([how it works](../#kako-radi)). Change the [carcass](./korpus.html) width — and the list changes by itself.

A list is short when it is right.

The front gap is the space between two neighbouring fronts, and between a front and the edge of the unit, that leaves room for the doors and drawers to open without rubbing. Without a gap the fronts touch, and every millimetre of uneven floor shows in the joint line.

## Standard sizes

- **3 mm** between two neighbouring fronts (program default).
- **1.5 mm** at each unit edge, so two neighbouring carcasses again give 3 mm between their fronts.
- Example: a [carcass](./korpus.html) 600 wide with one door → front **597 mm** (600 − 2 × 1.5); height 720 → front **717 mm**.
- Two doors on 800: 800 − 2 × 1.5 − 3 = 794, **397 mm** per door.
- With a [Gola profile](./gola-rucka.html) the gap is dictated by the profile; with [push-to-open](./push-sistem.html) it is larger by the ejector travel (about 2–3 mm).

## In the workshop

The gap is cut into the front, not left for installation: the front is always smaller than the carcass by the gap, and the doors are then adjusted on the hinge in depth, side and height so the joints are even. Common mistake: a front at the full carcass size (600 × 720), so the doors of neighbouring units rub and have to be cut, and cutting takes the edging off. Another: a 3 mm gap and then the edging thickness on top; the size in the cut list is net after edging, so 597 means 597 with the edging on. A third: a different gap on the wall and base rows, so the joint lines don't sit one above the other.

## How TOPICA handles it

The gap sits in one place in the settings (3 / 1.5 mm) and the program includes it in the size of every [front](./front.html) and in the cut list; when you change the front split, the gaps are recalculated ([how it works](../#kako-radi)).

Three millimetres is a small size, until it isn't there.

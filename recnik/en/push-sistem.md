Push-to-open is a mechanism that opens a front without a handle: you press the front, an ejector in the carcass pushes it out a few millimetres, and from there it opens by hand. It is used on smooth fronts when neither a handle nor a [Gola profile](./gola-rucka.html) is wanted.

## Standard sizes

- The mechanism to the manufacturer's catalog (e.g. Blum TIP-ON, Hettich Push to open); it is fitted in the carcass or on the side, with a [hinge](./sarka.html) **without a spring**, because a spring would hold the front shut and cancel the ejection.
- The front gap is larger by the ejector travel, **about 2–3 mm** more than usual, so the front doesn't hit its neighbour when it pops out.
- For drawers there are runners with a built-in push mechanism; sizes and gaps to the manufacturer's instructions.

## In the workshop

The ejector is placed opposite the edge of the front on the side away from the hinges; on tall doors two go in, top and bottom. A closed front has to sit flush with its neighbour, and that is adjusted on the ejector itself, not on the hinge. Common mistake: sprung hinges out of habit, so the front opens two millimetres and goes back. Another: push on a front that butts against a wall or a neighbouring unit at an angle, so there is no room for the ejector travel. A third: a push mechanism with the ordinary 3 mm gap, so the fronts rub against each other when they pop out. Before fitting, check the manufacturer's instructions for the exact travel and ejector position.

## How TOPICA handles it

When you choose push for a [front](./front.html), the program sets the gap by the mechanism instead of the default 3 mm; you enter the fittings and their price like everything else ([features](../#mogucnosti)).

A handleless front looks good while it works; that is why the gap and the hinge are chosen before cutting, not after.

A cam fitting (minifix) is hardware for joining two panels at a right angle: the cam housing in one panel grips a dowel bolt screwed into the end of the other, and the joint can be taken apart. A confirmat screw is driven through one panel straight into the end of the other; it is faster, but the head shows and the joint does not come apart cleanly. Both hold the [carcass](./korpus.html) together: side to bottom, side to top or rail.

## Standard sizes

- Cam housing **Ø15 mm**, centre **34 mm** from the panel edge for 18/19 mm board; for 16 mm board 24 mm — check against the hardware manufacturer's catalog.
- Dowel bolt **Ø8 mm** into the end of the neighbouring panel, with a **Ø8 × 30 mm** wooden dowel that locates the joint and stops it twisting.
- Confirmat **7 × 50 mm**: a **5 mm** hole in the end, **7 mm** through the panel.
- Positions are taken from [System 32](../blog/sistem-32.html), so they line up with the row of holes for shelves and hinges.

## In the workshop

The cam fitting is chosen where the joint is seen from inside (kitchen carcass, a wardrobe assembled at the customer's), the confirmat where speed counts more than looks (back rail, secondary parts) or when the head is covered with a cap. Common mistake with cam fittings: the housing drilled without a depth stop, so it breaks through the other face of the 18 mm board. With confirmats: the end hole without pre-drilling, so the screw splits the chipboard through the middle of its thickness. Both show up only once the panel is already edged.

## How TOPICA handles it

The program counts the fittings per carcass and puts them in the hardware list and the price, next to the panel sizes from the [cut list](../blog/krojna-lista.html); the hardware prices are entered by the tradesman ([how it works](../#kako-radi)).

Two cam fittings per joint, a dowel in between — and the carcass stands square.

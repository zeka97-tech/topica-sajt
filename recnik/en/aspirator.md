A cooker hood (extractor hood) is the appliance above the hob that draws off steam and smells; in a kitchen it is either integrated, inside a wall unit, or free-standing (wall-mounted, chimney type) between two wall units. The hood unit is a shortened wall [carcass](./korpus.html) that the appliance sits in, with a passage for the duct.

## Standard sizes

- Widths **600 and 900 mm**, the same as the hob below.
- Height of the hood's bottom edge above the hob: **650 mm** for an electric hob, **750 mm** for gas — according to the manufacturer's instructions.
- The bottom edge of the other wall units is at **1400 mm** from the floor (program default), which is about 500–550 mm above the worktop. Example: worktop at 858 from the floor, electric hob → bottom edge of the hood at 1508, so about 110 mm higher than the neighbouring wall units; with gas 1608. The rest: [standard kitchen sizes](../blog/standardne-mere-kuhinje.html).
- The duct runs through the wall unit or above it, to the wall or an outside vent.

## In the workshop

Before cutting the wall run, check what the hob is (gas or electric) and which hood it is, because the unit height and the duct cut-out in the top and back depend on it. Common mistake: all wall units the same height, so the hood ends up too low above a gas hob or the unit gets cut on site. Another: the duct cut-out made for one diameter while the appliance needs another, so power is lost on a reducer; take the duct size from the instructions.

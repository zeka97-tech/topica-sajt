The **top** is the upper horizontal panel of the carcass, the partner of the [bottom](./dno.html): it closes the box from above and holds the sides apart. Wall (hung) units, wardrobes, cabinets and tall units have one. Base kitchen units, which stand under the worktop, usually have no top: two [rails](./precka.html) go in its place.

## Standard sizes

- Chipboard **18 mm**, the same width and depth as the bottom of the same carcass: as wide as the unit when it sits over the sides, as deep as the side.
- Wall unit: height 720 (variants 360, 900/920), depth 300–350; top above, bottom below, both over the sides.
- Edging: **0.45 mm** on the front edge.

## Why base units have no top

Under the worktop a top has nothing to close: the 38 mm [worktop](./radna-ploca.html) lies across all the units and covers them itself. A full panel would waste material, and in a sink unit it would be in the way of the trap and the tap. Two **100 mm** rails, front and back, hold the sides apart, take the worktop screws and give the carcass stiffness. In a sink unit the back rail is left out, to leave room for the trap. That is why the cut list for a 600 base unit says “rail 564 × 100 ×2”, not top.

## In the workshop

The top is made the same way as the bottom: drilled as a pair with it, edged on the front and assembled before the back. On hung units the wall hanging hardware goes with the top, so the sides are not shortened at the top by guesswork. Common mistake: the tradesman gives a base unit a full top out of habit, then cuts an opening for the trap in it later and loses an hour.

## How TOPICA handles it

The wall unit template has a top, the base unit template two rails, and the sink unit only a front one; a full top on a base unit is one click away when you really need it ([features](../#mogucnosti)).

A top goes where something needs covering, and under the worktop that is already done.

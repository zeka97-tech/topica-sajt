A **divider** (partition) is an upright panel inside the carcass that splits the unit into two openings, left and right. It stands between the bottom and the top (or rails) as a third [side](./bok.html), only without an outer face. It makes room for two fronts, carries shelves on both sides and shortens the span on wide units.

## Standard sizes

- Chipboard **18 mm**; height as the internal opening of the carcass, depth as the side or set back like a shelf, depending on the design.
- Wide units, usually 1000 and 1200 mm, most often get a divider in the middle, so each opening has its own door or drawers; the same goes for a wardrobe with several doors.
- Holes for shelves and hinges to [System 32](../blog/sistem-32.html) on both faces, the first row at **37 mm** from the front edge.
- Edging: **0.45 mm** on the front edge.
- Hinges on a divider: two fronts share the same 18 mm panel, so **half overlay** hinges are chosen (9 mm overlay per front) and the mounting plates are staggered in height so they don't collide.

## In the workshop

The divider is drilled as a pair with the sides, from the same edge, and assembled with the bottom and top before the back. Its position is measured from the outer edge of the side to the centre line of the divider, so both openings are equal or exactly what the front needs. Common mistake: the divider placed in the middle of the internal opening while the fronts were worked out to the middle of the outside size, so the [3 mm gap](./front.html) between the doors doesn't line up; another is forgetting the shelf holes on the other face of the divider.

## How TOPICA handles it

You set the split in the carcass, and the program works out the divider, both openings and the fronts with their gaps; all the panels go into the cut list like the sides do ([features](../#mogucnosti)).

A divider is a side that does two jobs, so drill it for both.

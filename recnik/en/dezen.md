The decor is the pattern and colour of the decor paper on melamine-faced chipboard: white, grey, sonoma oak, walnut, concrete. A wood-pattern decor has a grain direction, the way the lines run, and it decides how the panel is turned on the sheet and how it is written in the cut list.

## Standard sizes

- In the cut list the **first dimension = grain direction**: a [front](./front.html) of 717 × 597 has its grain upright (along the height), while 597 × 717 would have it horizontal.
- Fronts: grain **upright**. Carcass: grain **along the length of the panel** (side along the height, bottom and shelf along the unit width).
- Hardboard and plain decors (white, grey): “no grain”; the panel is turned whichever way wastes less.
- Edging in the same decor: **1 mm** for the front, **0.45 mm** for the carcass.

## In the workshop

Before cutting, the grain direction is set on the sheet and all patterned panels are laid out that way, and only then the plain ones. For a stack of drawers one below the other, the fronts are often cut from one strip of the sheet, in order, so the pattern runs through the whole unit; for that the list has to know the drawer order. Common mistake: the two front dimensions swapped in the list, so on one door the grain runs horizontal and on the next upright; you only see it once the kitchen is installed. Another: [edging](./kant.html) from a different maker in the “same” decor that doesn't match the board under the light.

## How TOPICA handles it

You pick the decor per carcass or per front, and the program keeps the first-dimension rule in the [cut list](../blog/krojna-lista.html) and in 3D draws the pattern from the corner of the cut panel, the way it will sit when assembled ([how it works](../#kako-radi)).

A pattern that runs straight is the first thing the customer sees and the last thing they ask about.

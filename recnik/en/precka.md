The **rail** (stretcher) is a narrow horizontal panel between the sides, at the top of a base kitchen unit, where the [top](./krov.html) would otherwise be. There are two: a front one, directly behind the front, and a back one, against the back panel. They hold the sides at the exact spacing, stiffen the box, and the worktop is screwed into them from below.

## Standard sizes

- Chipboard **18 mm**, rail height **100 mm** (program default; the usual European practice).
- Length = the internal carcass width. For a 600 unit with 18 mm sides that is **564 mm**; the [cut list](../blog/krojna-lista.html) says “rail 564 × 100 ×2”.
- Edging: **0.45 mm**; the program edges both long edges, because the lower one is seen when the door is open.
- Sink unit: **no back rail**, so the trap and waste have room; the front one stays.
- Gola profile: the sink unit loses the front rail too, because the profile takes its place.

## In the workshop

The rails are set flush with the top edge of the sides and fixed with a cam fitting or confirmat through the side. The front one usually goes flat, so it doesn't get in the way of the top drawer; the back one can go on edge, for stiffness. Common mistake: the tradesman fits a back rail in the sink unit too, so the trap can't be fitted without cutting; another is a top [drawer](./fioka-i-vodjica.html) whose box hits the front rail, because the rail height wasn't allowed for in the opening.

## How TOPICA handles it

The base unit template carries two rails instead of a top, the sink unit one; the program makes sure a drawer box never passes through the rail above ([features](../#mogucnosti)).

Two 100 mm strips are the cheapest part of the carcass and the only one the worktop really needs.

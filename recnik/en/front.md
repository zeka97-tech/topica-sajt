The **front** is everything you see from the front: the door on hinges and the [drawer](./fioka-i-vodjica.html) front. A front carries nothing, but it decides the look, the price and half the work in the workshop. Its size is not the carcass size, but the carcass size less the gaps.

## Standard sizes

- Chipboard **18 mm** in a decor, or an MDF front of the same thickness.
- Gap: **3 mm** between two neighbouring fronts, **1.5 mm** at each unit edge (program default). For a 600 × 720 carcass the front is **717 × 597 mm**: 720 − 1.5 − 1.5 and 600 − 1.5 − 1.5.
- Edging: **1 mm** on all four edges; **2 mm** for heavily used fronts, e.g. wardrobe doors.
- The overlay, how far the front covers the side, depends on the [hinge](./sarka.html): full overlay 18, half overlay 9, inset 0 mm.
- Grain direction: in the cut list a front is written upright, first dimension = grain direction.
- Gola and push-to-open: gaps to the profile or the ejector travel (usually 2–3 mm more).

## In the workshop

Fronts are cut last, once the carcasses are assembled and measured, and edged on all sides before drilling for cups and handles. Doors are drilled for the Ø35 cup 100 mm from the top and bottom edge, and the handle to the same drawing for every front in the run. Common mistake: a front cut to the carcass size (720 × 600), so it won't fit between its neighbours; another is the grain turned, so one door in the run has its pattern running across.

## How TOPICA handles it

The front is derived from the carcass and the hinge: change the unit width or the hinge type and the front gets a new size, edging and number of cups, and the split into two doors is set in one place ([how it works](../#kako-radi)).

The front is the only thing the customer sees, so make it the last thing you cut and the first thing you check.

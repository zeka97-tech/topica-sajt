The **bottom** is the lower horizontal panel of the carcass, the one that ties the two [sides](./bok.html) together and carries everything that stands in the unit. On base kitchen units the legs are fixed to the bottom, so it also takes the weight of the worktop. It is the same thickness as the rest of the box, but the width of the whole carcass starts from it.

## Standard sizes

- Chipboard **18 mm**.
- Width: the full unit width when the bottom sits **over the sides** (TOPICA; the usual European practice). For a 600 unit the bottom is **600 × 539 mm**, and the sides are then 18 mm shorter.
- Depth: the same as the side, the carcass depth without front and back (560 − 18 − 3 = **539**).
- Edging: **0.45 mm** on the front edge; the other edges are hidden.
- Legs: adjustable plastic **100 mm** (80–150), **4 up to 800 mm wide, 6 above** (program default).

The other school puts the bottom between the sides: full-height sides, bottom 36 mm narrower. Both are correct; what matters is sticking to one through the whole kitchen, because the back and the shelves are worked out from it. The full carcass example is in the guide on the [cut list](../blog/krojna-lista.html).

## In the workshop

The bottom is drilled for cam fittings or confirmat screws against the ends of the sides and for the legs from below, then edged on the front before assembly. In a sink unit the bottom takes moisture, so the edging and joints get a closer look. Common mistake: the bottom cut to the inside size (564) even though the sides are shorter, or the other way round, so 36 mm are missing or sticking out across the width.

## How TOPICA handles it

In the program “bottom over sides” is the default: you enter the unit width, the bottom gets the full size and the sides are shortened by themselves ([how it works](../#kako-radi)). The program sets the number of legs by the width.

The bottom is the panel you start assembling from, so let it be the first one you check.

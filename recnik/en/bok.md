The **side** is the upright panel of the carcass, left and right. It carries everything: the hinge mounting plates, the drawer runners and the shelf supports sit on it, and the bottom, top or rails go into it. That makes the side the panel with the most drilling and the least room for error.

## Standard sizes

- Chipboard **18 mm**.
- Height: when the [bottom](./dno.html) sits over the sides (the program default), the side is 18 mm shorter. For a 720 carcass the side is **702 mm**.
- Depth: the carcass depth without front and back; for a 560 base unit that is **539 mm** (560 − 18 − 3).
- Holes to [System 32](../blog/sistem-32.html): a row of Ø5 mm holes at 32 mm centres, the first row **37 mm** from the front edge (centre line). Hinges, shelf supports and runners are all taken from it.
- Edging: **0.45 mm** on the front edge only. A visible outer side is not edged all round; it gets an 18 mm end panel in the front decor instead.

## In the workshop

Sides are cut in pairs and marked left and right straight away, because the hinge and runner holes mirror each other. When the side has a 10 mm deep rebate for the back, the rebate is routed before drilling. Common mistake: both sides drilled as left, so the hinge lands on the wrong side; another is a side cut to the full carcass height even though the bottom sits over it, so the unit sticks up 18 mm above its neighbour.

## How TOPICA handles it

The program derives both sides from the carcass sizes, shortens them by the bottom thickness itself and writes the edging on the front edge; in the [cut list](../blog/krojna-lista.html) they appear as “side ×2” ([features](../#mogucnosti)). The visible side at the end of a run gets an end panel that travels with the carcass.

The side is a mirror: whatever you do on the left, you do the other way round on the right.

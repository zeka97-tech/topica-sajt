The **plinth** (kickboard) is the board that closes the space between the floor and the bottom of the base units and hides the legs. It carries no load: the units stand on their legs, and the plinth clips onto them and comes off when you need to get at the floor or the services. A furniture plinth is not a skirting board, although people often mix them up.

## Standard sizes

- Height **100 mm** (80–150), the same as the legs; worktop height from the floor ≈ 720 + 100 + 38 = **858 mm** (see [worktop](./radna-ploca.html)).
- Board **18 mm** in the front decor; at least the top edge is usually edged. In a white or grey kitchen an aluminium profile often goes in instead of a board.
- **Set back** from the face of the front, so your toes don't hit it; in the program the setback is entered once and applies to the whole project.
- In **one line** along the whole run, without breaks; **always in front of the dishwasher**, because the machine has no front of its own down to the floor.
- On clips onto the legs; at a corner it is joined mitred or with a corner piece.

## In the workshop

The plinth is cut last, against the installed kitchen, in the full length of the run, and trimmed where the floor is not level. The clips are screwed on from the back exactly opposite the legs, and a rubber or brush seal goes along the bottom against water. Common mistake: plinth cut unit by unit, so a hole down to the floor is left in front of the dishwasher; another is a plinth flush with the [front](./front.html), so you kick it out every day.

## How TOPICA handles it

You build the plinth over the selected carcasses; the program gives it the leg height, the setback from the project and the front decor, and it travels with the carcasses when you move them ([features](../#mogucnosti)).

The plinth is the last board you cut and the first one the customer kicks, so set it back.

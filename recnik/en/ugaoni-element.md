The corner unit is the base carcass that closes the corner of a kitchen where two runs meet. There are two solutions: the blind corner, a wide unit of which one part stays behind the neighbouring run and has a front only on the visible part, and the L-corner, a unit with two legs and a bi-fold front right in the corner.

## Standard sizes

- Blind corner: width **1000–1200 mm**, front **450–600 mm**; the rest is the blind part that the neighbouring run stands in front of.
- L-corner: **900 × 900** or **1000 × 1000 mm**, a bi-fold front in the corner (two leaves at a right angle).
- Depth 560, height 720, plinth 100 as on the other base units; the [worktop](./radna-ploca.html) is joined in the corner mitred or with a joining strip.

## In the workshop

The blind corner needs a filler between its front and the first front of the neighbouring run, so the doors and handles open without colliding; the filler is cut in the front decor and appears in the cut list as a panel. The L-corner gives more usable space, but the hinges for the bi-fold front and the L-shaped shelf are more demanding to cut and fit. Common mistake: the corner measured as if the wall were square when it isn't, so the run doesn't sit right; see [how to measure a kitchen](../blog/kako-izmeriti-kuhinju.html). Another: the blind part without a shelf and support, so the 1200 mm bottom sags under load. A third: the filler forgotten, so the first door of the neighbouring run doesn't open fully.

## How TOPICA handles it

In the program's catalog the L-corner is a unit with legs, a shelf and a support; it is placed against the room's walls like any [carcass](./korpus.html) and goes into the cut list and the price with all its panels ([features](../#mogucnosti)).

The corner is the most expensive place in a kitchen when it is measured by eye.

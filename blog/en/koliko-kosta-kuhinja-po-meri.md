The price of a custom kitchen is not one number out of your head but the sum of five items: material, edging, hardware, labour and margin. Whoever works it out that way knows why the price is what it is and can defend it in front of the customer. Whoever works “per running metre” by eye does fine on a simple kitchen and loses on one with drawers and tall units. There are no prices in dinars or euros here, because every workshop has its own; here is the way those prices are put together.

## 1. Material: square metres of board, by type

First the square metres of each type of board are counted separately, because they are bought separately: white [chipboard](../recnik/iverica.html) 18 mm for the carcasses, 18 mm decor for the fronts, 3 mm hardboard for the backs and drawer bottoms, 38 mm worktop per running metre. The area comes from the [cut list](./krojna-lista.html): length × width × quantity, added up per material. An example from the program: one 600 × 720 × 560 base carcass has 8 panels and 2.34 m² of board, of which 1.48 m² white chipboard, 0.43 m² decor for the front and 0.43 m² hardboard.

That area is net. A sheet is never used whole: the usual sheet sizes are 2800 × 2070 mm or 2750 × 1830 mm, and after cutting there is waste from the saw cuts, the edges and the pieces that fit nowhere. Realistic **sheet yield is about 80 %**, so you divide the net square metres by 0.8 to get how much board you actually buy. A kitchen with 10 m² net of white chipboard needs about 12.5 m² of bought board. Whoever charges only the net pays for the waste out of their own pocket.

## 2. Edging: running metres plus waste

[Edging](../recnik/kant.html) is worked out per running metre, separately for each thickness, because the prices differ too: 0.45 mm for the visible carcass and shelf edges, 1 mm for fronts on all four edges, 2 mm for heavily used edges. The length is the sum of all edged edges from the cut list plus **10 % waste** for the start and end of the tape and for rejects. A 717 × 597 front alone carries 2.63 m of 1 mm edging; on twenty fronts that is over 50 m, which is not a small thing. The details are in the guide [on edging thickness and length](./kant-debljina-i-duzina.html).

## 3. Hardware: per piece, from the list

Hardware is counted, not estimated. The list for an ordinary kitchen: hinges (a front up to 900 mm high carries 2, up to 1600 three, up to 2000 four), runners or metal boxes per drawer, legs (4 up to 800 mm wide, 6 above), handles per front, cam fittings and dowels or confirmats per joint, plinth clips, wall-unit hangers, shelf supports. Each item has a price per piece from the supplier's catalog and a quantity from the design. Drawers are the most expensive here: one unit with three drawers on metal boxes costs more in hardware than three door units.

## 4. Labour: per unit or per m² of front

Two usual ways. **Per unit**: every carcass, base or wall, carries the same amount of labour, with an extra for the more complex ones (drawers, corner, oven). Simple and fast, good for straight kitchens. **Per m² of front**: labour is worked out on the area of the fronts, so a big wardrobe carries more than a small wall unit. More accurate for wardrobes and tall units. Whichever you choose, labour covers cutting, edging, drilling, assembly and a trial fit in the workshop. Installation at the customer's is a separate item, see below.

## 5. Margin

On the sum of material, edging, hardware and labour goes a **margin in per cent**. It is not the tradesman's earnings, because the labour is already paid; it covers what isn't seen in the design: tools, electricity, machines that wear out, days without work and the risk that something goes wrong. A workshop that works without a margin works for a wage, not for itself.

## What people forget

- **Transport**: boards from the supplier to the workshop, finished units to the customer, two van trips.
- **Installation**: a day or two at the customer's, with carrying upstairs, levelling, the sink and hob cut-outs to the manufacturer's template.
- **Waste**: that 20 % of the sheet and 10 % of the edging; and the hardboard, which everyone forgets because it is cheap.
- **Changes**: the customer who changes the decor three times or adds a drawer; every change is a new calculation and, if the board has already been cut, a new board.
- **Sundries**: screws, dowels, glue, silicone, worktop trims, wall trim; nothing on their own, noticeable together.
- **Services**: cutting and edging at someone else's if you don't have your own machines.
- **Appliances and worktop**: who supplies them, who fits them and who is responsible if the cut-out doesn't match.

Whoever has a list has no surprises; whoever doesn't sees them at the end of the job, when it is too late to charge for them.

## How TOPICA handles it

The program doesn't know your prices and doesn't invent them. In the **Workshop settings**, under “Materials and prices”, you enter the board price by type, edging per metre for each thickness (0.4; 0.45; 0.8; 1 and 2 mm), hardware per piece, **labour per unit** and the **margin** in per cent. A field you haven't filled in says “not entered” and counts as zero, without a warning, so the price in the report never contains a number you didn't give it. Once the prices are entered, for every project the program adds up the square metres by material, the metres of edging with waste, the hardware per piece and the labour per unit, adds the margin and in the Reports, “Price” tab, gives the price per project, next to the “Materials” and “Hardware” tabs where you see what it is made of. Change a unit width or add a drawer, and the price changes that instant. An overview of everything the program gives is in the section [Features](../#mogucnosti).

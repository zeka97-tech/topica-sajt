A drawer is a front plus a box plus a runner, and those three parts have to fit to the millimetre. The front is what shows, but the box and the runner decide whether the drawer glides or rubs. A 2 mm error per side means the box doesn't go in or it rattles. Here is the order in which a drawer is worked out in the workshop for an ordinary base [carcass](../recnik/korpus.html) 600 mm wide, with the three types of runner bought most often.

## The clear opening first, then everything else

All drawer sizes start from the **clear opening**: the distance between the inside faces of the sides. For a 600 mm carcass with 18 mm chipboard sides the opening is 600 − 2 × 18 = **564 mm**. For depth you work with the internal carcass depth: a carcass 560 deep has panels 539 mm deep, because the front (18) and the hardboard back (3) come off the 560. Don't measure the opening over the front and don't forget the back; those are the two most common mistakes before anything has been cut.

## Three types of runner and their clearance

The clearance is the space the runner needs between the box and the carcass side, on each side separately.

| Runner | Clearance per side | Lengths | What it carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ball-bearing telescopic | **13 mm** | 250–550, 50 mm steps | a wooden box (18 mm sides) |
| Roller | **12.5 mm** | 250–500 | a wooden box, lighter drawers |
| Metal box (Modern Box, PRO) | by the catalog formula | PRO: NL 300–550 | its own sides, you cut only the bottom and back |

With a wooden box you make the whole box: two sides, a front and back panel and a 3 mm hardboard bottom. With a metal box the sides are factory-made and you cut only the **bottom and back** to the manufacturer's formula. The formulas for the GTV Modern Box from the catalog: bottom width = clear opening − 86, bottom length = NL − 24, back = opening − 97, box heights 68/83/146/210. For the Modern Box PRO: opening − 75, NL − 24, opening − 87, heights 84/135/167/199.

## Runner length: the longest that fits

The rule is simple and the program keeps it the same way: **the runner is the longest nominal length that fits the carcass depth**. For an internal depth of 539 mm a 550 ball-bearing runner doesn't fit, so it is **500**. The same logic applies to the roller runner (500) and the metal box (NL 500). Whoever takes 550 because “there is room up to the wall” has forgotten the hardboard and the front; the runner then hits the back or pushes the front out.

## Example: a 600 × 720 × 560 carcass, three drawers

Opening 564 mm, internal depth 539 mm, runner 500.

**Ball-bearing telescopic, wooden box.** The outside width of the box is 564 − 2 × 13 = **538 mm**. The box sides are 18 mm chipboard, as long as the runner (500 mm, the usual practice). The front and back panels of the box go between the sides: 538 − 36 = **502 mm**. The 3 mm hardboard bottom is, by the program's rule, 3 mm narrower and shorter than the box: **535 × 497 mm**.

| Box panel | Size (mm) | Thk. | Qty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Box side | 500 × height | 18 | 2 |
| Front / back panel | 502 × height | 18 | 2 |
| Bottom | 535 × 497 | 3 | 1 |

**Roller.** A 12.5 mm clearance per side gives a box of 564 − 25 = **539 mm**, so the front and back panels are 503 mm. The rest as above.

**Modern Box.** Bottom 564 − 86 = **478 mm** wide and 500 − 24 = **476 mm** long; back 564 − 97 = **467 mm** wide. **Modern Box PRO**: bottom **489 × 476**, back **477** mm. The back height follows the box height you bought, from the catalog table.

## Box side height

The side height of a wooden box is chosen by the front height and by what goes in the drawer: for ball-bearing runners the usual ones are M 85, K 120, C 150 and F 180 mm, for roller runners 85/120/150. The top drawer under the rail usually gets the lowest box, the bottom one the highest. The rule the program won't let you break: **the box never goes through the panel above**. On a base unit under the worktop there are two 100 mm rails above the top drawer, so the top box has to fit under them, however tall the front is. [Drawers](../recnik/fioka-i-vodjica.html) are counted from the top, so the “first drawer” is always the one under the rail.

## Common mistakes

- Clearance deducted once instead of on both sides: a box of 551 instead of 538, it doesn't go in.
- Opening measured over the outside carcass width (600) instead of between the sides (564).
- Runner length chosen by the outside carcass depth (560) instead of the internal one (539).
- The metal box formula applied to the box width instead of the clear opening.
- Forgotten [front gap](../recnik/zazor-fronta.html): 3 mm between neighbouring fronts and 1.5 mm at each unit edge, so the drawer fronts rub against each other.
- Edging: a drawer front is edged on all four edges with 1 mm tape, while the sides of a wooden box get only the top edge in 0.45 mm; whoever edges the box all round changes its size. More in the guide [on edge banding](./kant-debljina-i-duzina.html).

## How TOPICA handles it

In the program you don't work the drawer out: on the carcass you click “Drawers” with plus and minus, enter how many there are, and the box, bottom and runner are derived from the clear opening and the depth by the rules above. The runner is GTV by default, the longest length that fits; when you change the carcass depth from 560 to 500, the runner and the box get new sizes, and with them the [cut list](./krojna-lista.html).

A drawer is the most expensive place for a mistake in a kitchen, because the mistake only shows once the runners are screwed on and the front is hung. That is why it is worked out before cutting, not after.

System 32 is the convention by which 5 mm holes are drilled in a row on the carcass side, at 32 mm centres, so that shelf supports, hinge mounting plates and drawer runners all land in the same holes. It is not a law or a stamped standard, but a convention kept by the hardware manufacturers (Blum, Hettich, Grass, GTV) and the makers of drilling machines. Whoever follows it can buy hardware from any of them and it will land in the holes already drilled.

## Why 32 mm of all things

The spacing wasn't chosen because it is a nice number. When panel furniture in Europe went into series production, it was drilled on multi-spindle boring machines: a row of spindles that drills the whole row of holes in one stroke. The spindles are driven by gears that touch each other, and the smallest centre distance the gears of the time could be packed into was 32 mm. The machine gave the grid, and the hardware manufacturers built their supports, mounting plates and runners around it. So the gear spacing became the furniture spacing and has stayed to this day, even on CNC machines that no longer need that reason.

The **37 mm** from the front edge of the [side](../recnik/bok.html) to the centre line of the first row is the second part of the convention. It is matched to the hinge mounting plate and the front end of the runner, so the same row of holes serves both shelves and hardware. The rear row is 37 mm from the rear edge or at 37 + n·32 from the front, so the runner lands on the grid at both ends.

## Three numbers to remember

- **Ø5 mm**: the hole diameter. The pin of a shelf support goes straight in, the hinge plate and the runner are screwed with a Euro screw made for a 5 mm hole.
- **32 mm**: the centre distance between neighbouring holes in a row. Not 30, not 35; any other spacing means a shelf sits crooked or the hardware doesn't fit.
- **37 mm**: the centre line of the front row from the front edge of the side. Measured from the edge of the finished side, after [edging](../recnik/kant.html).

## What is derived from the system

**Shelves.** The [shelf](../recnik/polica.html) support goes in any hole of the front and rear row, so the shelf moves in 32 mm steps. The customer moves the shelf themselves later, and it stays level because all four holes are at the same height.

**Hinges.** The [hinge](../recnik/sarka.html) mounting plate is screwed into the front row, at 37 mm. The Ø35 cup is drilled in the front to a depth of 12.5–13 mm, with its centre 21.5–22.5 mm from the front edge (distance K from 3 to 6 mm, to the hinge catalog). The number of hinges goes by front height: up to 900 mm two, up to 1600 three, up to 2000 four, above that five. The cups are 100 mm from the top and bottom edge of the front, the rest evenly between.

**Runners.** The front end of the runner goes in the front row, the rear end in the rear row. That is why the rear row has to be on the grid from the front one; if it isn't, the runner sits at an angle and the [drawer](../recnik/fioka-i-vodjica.html) rubs. The height at which the runner is screwed depends on the drawer system and is taken from the manufacturer's instructions, but it is always one of the holes in the row.

## How to drill without a CNC

You don't need a machine, you need a jig. The simplest is a strip of hardwood or aluminium with Ø5 holes at exactly 32 mm and a fence that sits against the front edge of the side so the first row lands at 37 mm. There are also bought jigs with steel bushes, which don't wear out in a few months like wooden ones. The procedure is always the same:

1. Side on the bench, inside face up, front edge towards you.
2. The jig's fence against the front edge; line the jig up in height the same way for every side in the project.
3. Drill with a depth stop; the side is 18 mm and there is no spare.
4. Drill the second side mirrored: front edge against the fence again, inside face up again.

The step that pays off most: make the jig longer than you need and drill the whole side in one go, without moving it. Every move is a chance for the row to drift by a millimetre.

## Common mistakes

- **Wrong edge.** The row drilled 37 mm from the rear edge instead of the front. The shelf fits, the hinge doesn't.
- **Sides not mirrored.** Both sides drilled from the same side, so one has its holes on the outside. Rule: inside face up, front edge against the fence, no exceptions.
- **Adding up with a tape.** Whoever measures 32, then another 32, then another 32, collects half a millimetre of error per hole. Measure from one zero or use a jig.
- **Drilled through.** Without a depth stop, the hole comes out on the show face. It hurts most on an [end panel](../recnik/ukrasna-strana.html) in the front decor.
- **A different zero on the two sides.** The jig on the left side registered from the bottom, on the right from the top. The holes are at 32 mm, but not at the same height.

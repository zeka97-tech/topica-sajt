Ten minutes is not an advert but a measure for an ordinary straight kitchen: five base units, five wall units, one wall. The first time takes longer, because you are looking for where things are. The third time takes less, because most things aren't done but only checked. This is the order of steps as the program offers them, with what you actually see on the screen.

## 1. New project and room sizes (1 minute)

On the start screen you choose the project type: **Kitchen**, **Wardrobe**, **Cabinet** or **Empty room**. Below it says the default room, 3000 × 4000 mm with a 2600 ceiling, and that it is changed in the **Room** tab. Once the project starts, you click “Room” in the top left corner of the 3D view and get the right-hand panel: the room sizes (3600 × 3000 in the screenshot, height 2600), **Services** (water, power, gas, hood, radiator, window; you pick the type and the wall, then “Add”) and **Room look** with the floor (parquet and laminate, tiles, concrete) and the walls (colour or tiles). Floor and wall are a matter of looks; the room sizes are a matter of construction, because the bottom bar constantly shows how long the base run is and how much wall is free.

![The “Room” panel in TOPICA: a 3600 × 3000 mm room 2600 high, choice of services per wall, grey laminate floor and tiles behind the kitchen, in 3D a run of base and wall carcasses](../slike/web/snimak-44-soba-pod-zid-1600.webp "Room: sizes, services and look on one panel. Bottom bar: base run 3200 mm, 400 mm free.")

## 2. The first base carcass (1 minute)

The **Add** button at the top of the screen or a click on the plus on the wall (“Click + on the wall or Add”, it says in an empty room). The first base carcass sits against the wall with the catalog sizes: **600 × 720 × 560**. The right-hand panel shows everything about it at once: width, height and depth in fields you change by typing; “Inside” with the door (1, full overlay hinge, hinges left), shelves and dividers; “Place” with the distance from the left wall, from the back wall and from the floor; “Look” with the carcass material (white chipboard), the front (oak decor), carcass edging 0.45 mm and front edging 1 mm. The dimensions “to neighbour”, “to back wall” and “from floor” sit on the carcass itself in 3D. On the left is the “Add to this carcass” tab: door, shelf, divider, handle, worktop, plinth, appliance, legs.

![A 600 × 720 × 560 mm base carcass with a door in TOPICA against the wall, with dimensions to the neighbour and from the floor, the size panel and the “Add to this carcass” tab](../slike/web/snimak-12-korpus-sa-vratima-1600.webp "The first carcass: sizes, door, hinge, edging and place against the wall, all on one panel.")

Once the first one is right, **Duplicate** from the top bar makes the next one with the same properties, and the program sets it against its neighbour. A new carcass inherits the material and edging from the one next to it. What every panel the program makes means is in the glossary under [carcass](../recnik/korpus.html).

## 3. Drawers (1 minute)

A drawer carcass has a **Drawers** row on the panel with minus and plus. Three drawers on a 600 unit: the box, bottom and runner are derived from the clear opening (564 mm) and the internal depth, by the rule that the runner is the longest length that fits. Drawers are counted from the top, and the top box never goes through the rail above. Working a drawer out on paper is in a separate guide; here one plus is enough.

![A 600 × 720 × 560 mm base carcass with three drawers in TOPICA, a panel with the number of drawers 3 and the “Add to this carcass” tab with drawer, divider, handle and end panel](../slike/web/snimak-13-korpus-sa-fiokama-1600.webp "Three drawers with one button; the program works out the box and the runner.")

## 4. The run, the wall row and the decor (3 minutes)

The base run is filled by duplicating and changing widths: in the screenshot the base run is 3200 mm, with 400 left free. The sink unit and the oven housing come from the same catalog, with their own rules (sink without a back rail, oven with a recess of at least 568 mm). You add the wall run with the same Add button; the wall units sit at the height from the project settings, bottom edge at 1400 mm from the floor. Then the [decor](../recnik/dezen.html): white chipboard for the carcass, oak or sonoma for the front, and that is changed in one place in “Look”, not on every unit separately.

![A straight kitchen in TOPICA: five base and five wall carcasses against the wall, sonoma decor fronts, white chipboard carcass, grey laminate on the floor](../slike/web/snimak-41-kuhinja-sonoma-bela-laminat-1600.webp "Base run 3200 mm, wall run at 1400 mm from the floor. The decor is chosen once for the whole run.")

## 5. Plinth and worktop (1 minute)

Both are added from the “Add to this carcass” tab: **Plinth** and **Worktop**. They are built over the selected carcasses and travel with them when you move them. The plinth has its own panel: sizes (600 × 100 × 18 in the screenshot), the **plinth setback** from the face of the front (50 mm) and the note “tied to: door unit, moves with it”. The plinth decor follows the fronts. The [plinth](../recnik/cokla.html) is always in front of the dishwasher and in one line; the [worktop](../recnik/radna-ploca.html) is 38 mm with an overhang past the front, so the overall depth goes from 560 to 600.

![The plinth panel in TOPICA: a 600 × 100 × 18 mm plinth under a base carcass, 50 mm setback from the face of the front, tied to the door unit](../slike/web/snimak-39-cokla-1600.webp "Plinth: set back 50 mm, decor like the front, moves with the carcass.")

## 6. The “By size” cut list (0 minutes)

The **Cut sizes** button at the top of the screen opens the Reports: tabs Cut sizes, **By size**, Hardware, Materials, Price and Report / PDF. The list isn't “made”; it is already there and says “updated live”. At the top are the summary cards: number of carcasses, number of panels, board area. For one 600 × 720 × 560 carcass that is 8 panels and 2.34 m² of board. Below is the table by material with the size (the first dimension is the grain direction), thickness, edging and quantity. **Copy** takes the table into a message, **Print / PDF** onto paper, **CSV** into a spreadsheet. How that list is read is explained in the guide [What a cut list is](./krojna-lista.html).

![The “By size” report in TOPICA: the 8 panels of one carcass by material, white chipboard, oak decor and hardboard, with sizes, thickness, edging and quantity](../slike/web/snimak-33-po-meri-1600.webp "“By size”: identical sizes on one line, ready to copy.")

## 7. Price (1–2 minutes the first time, then 0)

The price comes out of what you entered once in the **Workshop settings**, group “Materials and prices”, section “Price and quote”: labour per unit, margin in per cent, edging per metre for each thickness (0.4; 0.45; 0.8; 1; 2 mm), the hood duct and cover plate. While a field says “not entered”, the program counts zero and invents nothing. Once the prices are in, the Price tab in the Reports gives the price per project without a single extra click.

![The workshop settings in TOPICA, section “Price and quote”: fields for labour per unit, margin and edging per metre for 0.4 to 2 mm, empty fields marked “not entered”](../slike/web/snimak-37-cena-1600.webp "The prices are entered by the tradesman, once; from then on the program adds them up per project.")

## Total

Room 1, first carcass 1, drawers 1, run and decor 3, plinth and worktop 1, prices 1–2. About ten minutes for a straight kitchen, provided the room has been measured and you know what the customer wants. What the program doesn't shorten is the conversation with the customer; what it shortens is everything that goes on paper after it. How that fits into the rest of the job is in the section [How it works](../#kako-radi).

The best test is your own next project: the same sizes, the same wall, and see how long it takes you this time.

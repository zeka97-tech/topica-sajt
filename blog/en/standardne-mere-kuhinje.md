Standard kitchen sizes are not a regulation but a convention every design starts from: the height at which it is comfortable to work, the depth that takes a worktop and an appliance, the widths that go with a chipboard sheet and with built-in appliances. Whoever knows them by heart draws faster and makes fewer mistakes; whoever doesn't builds a kitchen that is technically correct and comfortable for nobody. Here are the sizes TOPICA uses as defaults, with the range the trade considers usual.

## Table: the sizes asked for most often

The worktop height from the floor and the gap to the wall units are sizes that are **adjusted to the user's height**: 858 is the sum of the default parts, not a rule. A tall person gets 900 or 920 by raising the plinth or the carcass, a short one goes to 850 by lowering the plinth. The program keeps these sizes in the project settings, so you change them once for the whole base run.

## Base units

The base carcass is 720 high and 560 deep. The bottom sits over the sides, so the side is 702 and the panel depth 539 (560 without the 18 front and the 3 back). Under the worktop there is no top but two 100 mm [rails](../recnik/precka.html), front and back; the sink unit has no back one, so the trap fits. The legs are four up to 800 mm wide, six above that. The width goes from 150 to 1200, and in practice it is picked from the 300 to 1200 series, because appliances and hardware follow those numbers: dishwasher 600, oven in a unit with a recess of at least 568 mm, sink 600 to 1000.

## Wall units

The wall carcass is also 720 high, 300 to 350 deep, with a top instead of rails. A lower 360 wall unit goes above the fridge or the hood, a taller 900/920 one when the ceiling allows and the customer wants more space. The bottom edge of the wall units sits at 1400 mm from the floor, which with the worktop at 858 gives about 540 mm of free wall; tiles, sockets and the [hood](../recnik/aspirator.html) fit in that gap, and the hood goes 650 mm above an electric hob and 750 above gas, to the manufacturer's instructions.

## Tall units and fronts

Tall units are 2000 to 2200 mm: for a built-in fridge, for an oven with a microwave at eye level, for a larder with shelves. Their top edge is usually lined up with the wall run, so the height depends on where the wall run is, not the other way round.

The fronts follow the carcass with a [gap](../recnik/zazor-fronta.html): 3 mm between neighbouring fronts and 1.5 mm at each unit edge. The front of a 600 × 720 base carcass is therefore 597 × 717, and with Gola and push-to-open the gaps follow the profile and the mechanism.

## When to deviate

- **The user's height.** A worktop at 858 is for the average person; whoever cooks bent over gets back pain, whoever cooks with raised elbows can't press down on the knife. Ask who cooks most and how tall they are, then adjust the plinth or the carcass height.
- **Low ceiling or a slope.** A 720 wall run at 1400 needs 2120 mm; below that goes a lower wall unit or a smaller gap.
- **A window on the wall.** A sill lower than the worktop sets the worktop height, not the other way round; the wall unit is left out there.
- **Appliances.** The oven recess, the dishwasher height with its feet and the fridge depth are sizes from the manufacturer's instructions and beat any table.
- **A deeper worktop** (620/635) when pipes run behind the carcasses or the wall is crooked; the overhang grows, the carcass stays 560.
- **An old flat.** A wall that is 20 mm out over three metres eats one standard width; that calls for a made-to-measure unit or an end panel that covers the gap.

## How TOPICA handles it

All the sizes in the table are project defaults: the base run height, plinth, worktop thickness and wall run height are entered once, and every new carcass inherits them. When you change the plinth from 100 to 120, the whole base run rises, the dimensions in the 3D view show the new numbers, and the [cut list](./krojna-lista.html) stays the same because the carcass hasn't changed. A made-to-measure unit you type straight into width, height and depth, as in the section [features](../#mogucnosti).

Standard sizes are a good start and a bad finish: start from them, and finish with the person who will stand in that kitchen every day.

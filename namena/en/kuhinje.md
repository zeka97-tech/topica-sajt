A kitchen is the most demanding job in panel furniture: the most units, the most types (sink, oven, corner, hood) and a customer who changes decisions right up to installation day. TOPICA was built for exactly that job. You draw the room, line up the carcasses along the wall, and the program keeps the construction, the cut list and the price right while you talk to the customer.

## The room first, then the run along the wall

You enter the walls, the ceiling height, the window and where the water and power are. Then you drop carcasses from the catalog: they snap to the wall and to their neighbour, and you change sizes live, to the millimetre. The standard sizes are already in the program: a base unit **720** high on **100 mm** legs, **560** deep under a 600 worktop, the wall run with its bottom edge at **1400 mm from the floor**. If your workshop works differently, you change it once in the project settings, not on every carcass. How the room is measured before that is in the guide [how to measure a kitchen](../blog/kako-izmeriti-kuhinju.html), and the numbers are collected in [standard kitchen sizes](../blog/standardne-mere-kuhinje.html).

## The special units every kitchen has

A kitchen is not a row of identical boxes. The program's catalog has the units that are built differently in the workshop:

- [Sink unit](../recnik/sudopera-element.html): no back rail, so the trap has room; the front rail stays.
- [Oven housing](../recnik/element-za-rernu.html): the recess for a built-in oven needs at least 568 mm; the program tells you if the recess doesn't fit.
- [Corner unit](../recnik/ugaoni-element.html): an L with two legs, a shelf and a support.
- [Cooker hood](../recnik/aspirator.html): bottom edge 650 mm above the hob for electric, 750 for gas, to the manufacturer's instructions.
- [Plinth](../recnik/cokla.html) and [worktop](../recnik/radna-ploca.html): built over the selected carcasses, in one line, and they travel with them when you move them. The plinth is set back and always in front of the dishwasher.

## Example: a 3200 mm base run

The wall is 3200 long. You place an 800 sink, a 600 oven housing, 600 drawers and two 600 door units. Above them four 800 wall units. For every carcass the program derives the panels: sides, bottom over the sides, two rails under the worktop, a 3 mm hardboard back, a front with 1 mm edging. It places the hinges by front height (up to 900 mm two), takes the runners by carcass depth, four legs per unit up to 800 mm wide.

Then the customer says the drawers should be 450 and the doors next to them 750. You change two widths. Every panel in those two carcasses gets a new size, the edging and hardware are recalculated, the worktop and plinth stay in one 3200 line. There is no “recalculate” command; [that is how the program works](../#kako-radi) from the start.

## What you get at the end

For the whole project: a cut list per carcass and per material, the “By size” view for copying and sending to the saw, a hardware list, board usage and a price at your prices for boards, edging, hardware and labour. All of it can go to PDF. How the list is read and why the first dimension is the grain direction is explained in the article on the [cut list](../blog/krojna-lista.html); the full list of what the program does is in [features](../#mogucnosti).

A kitchen is still made by hand. The program only makes sure every one of those hundred numbers is right when you start cutting.

In a small workshop the tradesman is designer, cutter, fitter and bookkeeper all at once. Software must not be a fifth job on top of those four. That is why TOPICA was built to be learned in one afternoon and to run on the laptop already sitting on the bench, without internet, accounts or training.

## A laptop on the bench

You need Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), 4 GB of memory and graphics that can run 3D in a browser; that is an ordinary workshop laptop. The program works without internet and creates no account. A project is one file on your computer: copy it to a USB stick, e-mail it to a colleague or save it in the customer's folder. Installation is three steps described on the [download](../preuzimanje.html) page.

## No training: three steps

There is no catalog to set up before the first project. You enter the room, drop a carcass from the catalog against the wall, read the cut list. The program knows the construction: sides, bottom over the sides, two rails under the worktop, back, front, hinges by front height, System 32 for the shelves. All the sizes follow the European standard you work to anyway: carcass 720, plinth 100, depth 560, edging 0.45 on the carcass and 1 mm on the front. Those [three steps](../#kako-radi) for the first carcass take five minutes, and that is the first cut list you can send to the saw.

## Your sizes and prices, entered once

A small workshop has its habits: back thickness, front edging, plinth height, runner supplier. You enter those once in the workshop settings and they apply to every project from then on. The same goes for prices: board per m², edging per metre, hardware per piece, labour per unit and margin. The program adds up per project, and a price not entered is zero; it invents nothing on your behalf.

Example: a customer asks for a quote for a kitchen with six base and five wall units. You put it together in half an hour, and the program immediately shows the board usage, the metres of edging (with 10 % waste), the hardware list and the price with your margin. You print the quote to PDF and send it the same day, while the customer is still thinking of you. How a kitchen price is put together is in the guide [how much a fitted kitchen costs](../blog/koliko-kosta-kuhinja-po-meri.html).

## When the customer changes their mind

In a small workshop there is nobody to redo the paperwork. On Thursday the customer wants drawers instead of doors, on Friday an 800 sink instead of 600. In the program you change the carcass, and everything that depends on it changes: panels, edging, hinges, runners, plinth, worktop, price. There is no “recalculate” command and no old list lagging behind in a folder. What goes into such a list and why the first dimension is the grain direction, read in the [glossary under cut list](../recnik/krojna-lista.html), and the whole way from the room to the list in the [ten-minute guide](../blog/od-prostora-do-krojne-liste-10-minuta.html).

## What the program doesn't do

It doesn't cut for you and it doesn't make a CNC file: this version has no machine export and no cutting plan (nesting). It gives an exact list, hardware and price; cutting and assembly stay yours.

The trial is free, so the simplest thing is to take the next small job and do it in the program instead of on paper.

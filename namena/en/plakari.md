A wardrobe has fewer types of unit than a kitchen, but it is stricter on sizes. It is 2000 to 2200 mm tall, the doors are big and heavy, and three millimetres of error in height show as a crooked gap from top to bottom. TOPICA builds a wardrobe like any other [carcass](../recnik/korpus.html): you give the sizes and the split, the program derives the panels, hinges, shelves and price.

## The wardrobe carcass and the split inside

The wardrobe depth is **600 mm** (carcass 580 + front 18). The height follows the room: you enter the ceiling height and the wardrobe fits under it, with dimensions from the floor always visible. You split the width with dividers; each section gets its own shelves on supports to System 32, a hanging rail at the usual 1700–1900 mm from the floor or internal [drawers on runners](../recnik/fioka-i-vodjica.html), which the program counts from the top. The back is 3 mm hardboard, and on a wardrobe standing free in the room the program makes it edge to edge, on the outside.

## Hinged doors: hinges by height

A tall [front](../recnik/front.html) needs more hinges, and that is where tradesmen most often save in the wrong place. The program keeps the rule: up to 900 mm high two hinges, up to 1600 three, up to 2000 four, above that five. The cup is Ø35, and the end hinges go 100 mm from the top and bottom edge of the front, the rest evenly between. The gap is 3 mm between neighbouring doors and 1.5 mm at the unit edge. Edging on wardrobe doors is 2 mm, because they are the hardest-working edges in the house; how the tape thickness is chosen is under [edge banding](../recnik/kant.html).

Example: a wardrobe 2000 wide, 2200 high, 600 deep, split into four sections of 500. Each door is between 1600 and 2000 mm high, so it gets four [hinges](../recnik/sarka.html): sixteen hinges, sixteen mounting plates, all in the hardware list before you go to the shop. Change the split to five sections of 400, and the list and the cut list are rearranged to the new layout at once.

## Sliding doors: numbers from practice

The wardrobe in the program's catalog is on hinged doors. If the customer wants sliding ones, you build the carcass, dividers, shelves and drawers in the program the same way, and work out the doors by the rule of the system you buy. For the PKM 80 it is: door height = opening − 40, overlap of neighbouring doors 40 mm, the tracks need 85 mm of depth, no door wider than about 1100 mm and up to 60 kg per door. On the wardrobe in the example that is two doors of about 1020 mm; check the track and overlap sizes against the manufacturer's instructions before cutting.

## What the program gives you for a wardrobe

A cut list grouped by material, with the rule that the first dimension is the grain direction: on tall doors with an oak or walnut pattern that is the difference between a wardrobe that looks like furniture and one that looks like a mistake. With it the hardware list (hinges, shelf supports, runners), board usage and the price at your prices. What the list looks like and how it is read, see the [cut list guide](../blog/krojna-lista.html); the whole procedure from the room to the list is in [three steps](../#kako-radi).

A wardrobe is the job where measuring twice pays most; the program measures every time.

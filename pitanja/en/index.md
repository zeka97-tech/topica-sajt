These are the questions tradesmen actually type into a search engine and post in groups when they look for kitchen design and cut list software, and the ones that reach us after the trial is downloaded. The answers are short and honest, including what the program doesn't do in this version.

![The “By size” view in TOPICA: a cut list for a 600 × 720 × 560 mm base carcass, eight panels by material with size, thickness, edging and quantity](../slike/web/snimak-33-po-meri-1600.webp "A cut list for one carcass, the kind most of the questions below are about.")

## How much does the TOPICA kitchen software cost?

The trial is free, with no account and no card. We haven't announced the price of the full version yet; as soon as it is known it will be in the [Pricing](../#cena) section, and there you can leave your e-mail to get one message when it comes out. Until then you use the trial on your own projects and see whether it suits you before you pay anything.

## Does it run on a Mac or on a phone?

No. TOPICA is a program for Windows 10 and Windows 11, 64-bit. There is no version for Mac, Android or iPhone and it doesn't run in a browser. An ordinary workshop laptop with 4 GB of memory and graphics that can run 3D in a browser is enough. You can take the project on the laptop to the customer and open the 3D with sizes on the spot.

## Do I need internet or an account for the program to work?

Neither. The program works without internet, creates no account and sends your projects nowhere. A project is one file on your computer: copy it to a USB stick, e-mail it to a colleague or save it in the customer's folder. You only need internet to download the program and, now and then, a new version from the [download](../preuzimanje.html) page.

## Does the program generate a cut list?

Yes, that is its main job. You don't write the cut list: it is calculated from the carcasses you placed in the room, per carcass and per material, with size, thickness, edging and quantity. When you change a unit's width, every panel gets new sizes at once. What the list looks like and how it is read is in the guide [What a cut list is](../blog/krojna-lista.html).

## How do I send the cut list to be cut?

The “By size” view puts identical sizes on one line, grouped by material, with edging and quantity. You copy it with one click and send it as a message or e-mail to the service that cuts for you, or print it to PDF. The first dimension is always the grain direction, so the service doesn't guess which way a front is turned. Sizes are net, after edging; tell the service that in your first message.

## Is there CNC export or a cutting plan (nesting)?

Not in this version. The program doesn't make a file for a CNC machine or an optimised layout of panels on the sheet. It gives an exact cut list, board usage in m² and edging length with 10 % waste, which is what a service or your own saw needs. If CNC export is essential for you, this is not the program for you today; we say that openly so you don't waste your time.

## Which hardware does the program know?

The program works to the European standard: frameless carcass, System 32 (a row of Ø5 holes at 32 mm centres, 37 mm from the front edge), hinges with a Ø35 cup, the number of hinges by front height (up to 900 mm two, up to 1600 three, up to 2000 four). Drawers are to the GTV catalog by default: a wooden box on ball-bearing runners or a Modern Box metal box; the runner is always the longest length that fits the carcass depth.

## Can I use my own board decor?

You can. Besides the decors offered (white, grey, beige, black, wood, concrete, marble, stainless) you add a photo of the board you actually buy and it shows on the fronts and carcasses in 3D. With patterned decors the program keeps the rule that the pattern runs from the corner of the cut panel, and in the cut list the first dimension is the grain direction, so 3D and the list show the same thing.

## Can it do a sliding wardrobe?

The wardrobe in the program's catalog is on hinged doors, with hinges by front height. You build the carcass, dividers, shelves and internal drawers of a sliding wardrobe the same way, and work out the sliding doors by the rule of the system you buy: for the PKM 80 the door height is the opening − 40, door overlap 40 mm, the tracks need 85 mm of depth, no door wider than about 1100 mm. Check against the track manufacturer's instructions.

## Can I make a PDF quote for the customer?

You can. You enter your prices for boards, edging, hardware and labour per unit and your margin, and the program adds up per project and gives the price. Everything you see on screen, including the cut list, hardware list and price, you print to PDF and send to the customer. How a kitchen price is put together and what goes into it is in the guide [How much does a fitted kitchen cost](../blog/koliko-kosta-kuhinja-po-meri.html).

## Which boards and thicknesses does the program support?

By default: 18 mm chipboard for carcass and front, 3 mm hardboard for the back, 0.45 mm edging on the carcass and 1 mm on the fronts. You change thicknesses and edging in the workshop settings and the change applies to every panel in the project. The usual sheet sizes (2800 × 2070 or 2750 × 1830 mm) you use for board usage; the program also works with 16, 19 and 25 mm board.

## Can I use 16 mm board?

You can: change the board thickness in the workshop settings and the program derives every panel with 16 mm, including the bottom over the sides and the carcass depth. Watch the hardware: with a cam fitting (minifix) the housing centre for 16 mm board is not 34 mm as for 18, but about 24 mm, which you should check against the hardware manufacturer's catalog before drilling.

## How do I change the carcass height for all units at once?

The default sizes sit in the project settings, not on each carcass: base unit 720 high and 560 deep, plinth 100, the wall run with its bottom edge at 1400 mm from the floor. Change them there and new carcasses follow, and every new carcass against the wall inherits its sizes from its neighbour. You can still change an individual unit separately, to the millimetre.

## Does the program remember my prices?

Yes. You enter the prices for boards, edging, hardware and labour and your margin once in the workshop settings and they apply to every project from then on until you change them. The program doesn't invent prices: whatever you haven't entered counts as zero, without a warning. That way the project price always shows exactly what you entered, not a penny more.

## What if the customer changes their mind after the cut list?

You change the carcass, not the paperwork. When the 600 drawers become 450 or the 600 sink becomes 800, the panels, edging, hinges, runners, plinth, worktop and price change that instant. There is no “recalculate” command and no old list lagging behind in a folder. You send the new list to be cut and that is all.

## Can I use the program on more than one computer?

You install the trial on as many computers as you like: no account, no activation, no per-device limit. A project is a file, so you open it on the laptop at the customer's and on the computer in the workshop. The terms for the full version, including the number of computers, will be announced together with the price in the [Pricing](../#cena) section.

## How is it installed and why does Windows show a blue screen?

You download the file TOPICA-trial.exe and run it with a double click. The program has no digital signature yet, so on first launch Windows shows a blue “Windows protected your PC” screen; that is normal for new, unsigned programs. You click “More info”, then “Run anyway”. The three steps are described on the [download](../preuzimanje.html) page.

## Is the program available in German, Croatian or English?

The program is currently in Serbian and English; you switch the language inside the program. German, Croatian and the other languages are not in the program yet, although the website has pages in them; those languages are coming. If you work for customers abroad, the English interface and a PDF quote in English are available to you right now.

## Does the program know System 32 and where the hinges are drilled?

It does. Shelves and hinge mounting plates follow System 32: a row of Ø5 holes at 32 mm centres, the first row 37 mm from the front edge of the side. The hinge cup is Ø35, the end hinges 100 mm from the top and bottom edge of the front, the rest evenly spaced. The program gives the number and layout of hinges by front height, and the cup drilling depth (12.5–13 mm) you take from the manufacturer's instructions.

## 1 mm or 2 mm edging, which does the program use?

By default 0.45 mm on the visible edges of carcass and shelves, 1 mm on all four edges of a front, and 2 mm for heavily used edges such as wardrobe doors. You change that in the workshop settings and it applies to every panel. The program writes the edging mark next to every panel in the cut list and adds up the tape length with 10 % waste; the price per metre is yours to enter.
